## (a) Which operator, and what an amplitude-like $\psi$ would need

"Amplitude-like" (LT1): $\psi$ superposes, evolves linearly and interferes (so it can cancel). Perron–Frobenius makes $\psi$ real, positive and unique up to scale for a nonnegative irreducible finite matrix (on an infinite state space neither existence nor uniqueness is automatic, which bears on O1 and on O2 without (a)), with $\psi^2$ MERW's stationary density when the operator is the symmetric adjacency matrix of a finite connected graph, the only case Burda et al. (2009) treat (§9). On a directed irreducible graph the same construction, $P_{ij}=\mathcal A_{ij}\psi^{\rm R}_j/(\lambda\psi^{\rm R}_i)$, has stationary density proportional to $\psi^{\rm L}_i\psi^{\rm R}_i$, the product of the left and right Perron vectors (Parry's measure of maximal entropy; Parry 1964, §3 Lemma 4 and Theorem 5, §4 Theorem 10: for a finite transitive 0/1 structure matrix $\Sigma$, not assumed symmetric, with simple Perron eigenvalue $\beta$ and positive right and left eigenvectors $l$ and $h$, the chain with transitions $\sigma(i,j)\,l(j)/(\beta\,l(i))$ has stationary probabilities $h(i)\,l(i)$ (normalised so that they sum to 1), and it is the only stationary process compatible with $\Sigma$ whose entropy equals the maximal value $\log\beta$). That is the square of a Perron vector only where the two coincide: always for a symmetric operator, and sometimes otherwise (a regular digraph, for example). O2's flagged state graph is never symmetric when $R$ has a type-(ii) edge: the converse of a jump arc would have to be the arrival state's one out-arc, a segment from target to source, and the edge would then be backward or type (i) (§5). So on O2 the two vectors coincide only if $R$ makes them; a square by symmetry needs O3. Positivity excludes cancellation among nonnegative combinations, the only ones a nonnegative operator produces from nonnegative data: $(a\psi_1+b\psi_2)^2\ge a^2\psi_1^2+b^2\psi_2^2$ for $a,b\ge0$.

## (c) Cheap probes, run

R1–R3: operator O2 on the (a)-restricted rule from the $r=10$ periastron of $[10,20]$, uniform weight on length-$L$ walks from the root (C36's root count). R4: small graphs, O3 and O5. Enumeration, no sampling.

- **R1, the spectrum (LT1 and LT3).**
  - At $c=0.01$ (87,356 states, 29,119 orbits) the strongly connected components are the 29,119 orbit 2-cycles and the 29,118 jump-arrival states, which are singletons (58,237 in all); jumps are acyclic (C33). The spectral radius is 1, so the entropy rate is 0. Eigenvalue 1 has algebraic multiplicity 29,119, one per 2-cycle ($-1$ likewise, from period 2), geometric multiplicity 14,478, one per stuck orbit, and index 15, the longest chain of 2-cycles (the root and 14 jumps). The nonnegative right eigenvector is unique up to scale but supported on the root orbit's two segment states alone; the nonnegative left eigenvectors form a cone whose extreme rays, one per stuck orbit, are supported on those orbits. Every left–right product vanishes, so **MERW's density ($\psi^2$ for a symmetric operator, $\psi^{\rm L}\psi^{\rm R}$ for a directed one) is undefined here**. There is as yet no $\psi$ here to check for injection; a future claim through O2 would first have to supply an $R$ whose state graph has positive left and right Perron vectors (an irreducible one has them).
  - Walk counts grow polynomially: $\log_2(N_{2000}/N_{1000})=14.185$, tending to 14 (longest chain 14 jumps; index 15); $0.999$ at $c=0.1$, where $N_L=L+1$ exactly (3 orbits, chain 1).
- **R2, the long-path count (LT3, cut depth).**
  - $c=0.01$: the root's jump-arm share is 0.400, 0.381974, 0.381966, 0.308087, 0.137830, 0.011828, 0.005865 at $L=2$, 10, 20, 50, 100, 1000, 2000, counting $L$ arcs after the first step (C36's root count at length $L+1$; at length $L$, the convention of the setup line of (c) and of the concentration and growth figures, the shares are $1/3$, $55/144=0.381944$, 0.381966, 0.314054, 0.139361, 0.011839, 0.005868). At length $L$ they are exactly the Fibonacci ratios $F_L/F_{L+2}$ for $L\le28$, which tend to $1/\varphi^2=0.381966$. Seven segment states with no admissible jump lie 28 arcs from the root, so from $L=29$ the shares depart from these ratios, and they then fall toward 0. The walks' jump count concentrates on 14 (0.8931 at $L=200$; 0.9909 at $L=2000$).
  - $c=0.1$: jump share 0.000500 at $L=2000$; concentration on 1 jump (0.9995).
  - **A MERW- or count-based claim at this cut would not be robust (LT3): the share is not stable in $L$ and tends to 0 at both values of $c$ (about $11.6/L$ at $c=0.01$; at $c=0.1$ exactly $1/(L+1)$ at length $L$); the longest chain, which depends on $c$, sets the growth of the walk count ($L^{14}$ against $L$) and the jump count on which walks concentrate.**
- **R3, the classifier (a check on the computation, not LT3's grain; see the LT3 row).** `kr.classify` against `c33r.py`: one state differs (87,357 against 87,356). Under `kr.classify` the target $r=17.14796096$ of a jump from the apastron $r=16.900001$ of $[16.158567,16.900001]$ is labelled a plunge, whose range $[0,17.147961]$ contains the source, so (a) admits a jump to a dead end; `c33r.py` labels it a near-circular bound orbit $[17.147937,17.147961]$, which (a) rejects. In 40-digit arithmetic on the same $(E,L)$ the target's orbit is bound, with turning points $17.14793743$ and $17.14796089$; the computed target lies $6.7\times10^{-8}$ beyond the outer one (double-precision rounding), and `kr.classify` then takes that outer root as the lower turning point and returns a plunge. The bound label is the one the exact orbit has, so C33's 14,478 is the count under it. That one extra admissible edge is the whole difference: it adds the dead-end state, makes its source a decision state, and leaves 14,477 orbits with no admissible edge under `kr.classify` against C33's 14,478 (`r3settle.py`). Shares agree to 6 decimals, jump-count laws to within one unit in the fourth significant figure. **The computed shares do not depend on the classifier. This checks the computation; it is not a test of a claim's robustness under LT3.**
- **R4, composition on toys (LT1).** Two $P_4$ joined end to end by an edge (that is, $P_8$; an end-to-inner join gives 0.935064, and an inner-to-inner join 0.967549): $\psi$ has overlap 0.933865 with $(\psi_1+\psi_2)/\sqrt2$, a fixed symmetric sum. $P_4$ joined to $K_4$ localises, a toy analogue of the localisation Burda et al. (2009) show on weakly diluted lattices: $\psi^2$ mass on $P_4$ is 0.042369, then $5.68\times10^{-3}$, $7.60\times10^{-4}$, $1.02\times10^{-4}$ with 1–3 bridge vertices ($P_4$ attached by an end vertex; bridge vertices not counted). $K_4$ already holds 0.957631 with no bridge, and the mass off $K_4$ stays near 0.0425 as the bridge grows, so the falling sequence is decay along the bridge; the localisation itself is winner-take-all rather than superposition. Disjoint $P_4\sqcup P_4$: degenerate, $\psi$ not unique. $C_4$ with flux $\theta=0,\pi/2,\pi$ (O5): $\mathrm{tr}\,H^4=32,24,16$; cancellation appears only once phases are supplied. A future LT1 claim through O3 would have to show interference that these toys do not; the degenerate disjoint toy does superpose (every $a\psi_1+b\psi_2$ is a leading eigenvector) but, with disjoint supports, cannot cancel.

**Limits.** One unmotivated rule (C26), one instance; a walk-length cut, not proper time; dead ends would drop out, where v0.9 §9's convention keeps them in a cut (none occur on the instance: every state has an out-arc); without (a) the reachable graph is countably infinite (C5, C29) and its completed histories form a continuum (C16). **Not run:** an O3 (symmetrised) census, minutes of compute. Its added arcs are the converses of jump edges, type-(ii) pairs that no rule supplies and that fail (a) on the instance, plus arcs that break the arrival flag (O3 row).
