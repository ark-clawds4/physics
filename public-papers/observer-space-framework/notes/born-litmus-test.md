## The question

> How best do we tell whether the Born rule genuinely emerges from the framework, rather than being written in by hand? (the litmus test)

David calls this an important question to answer.

## Working answer (L3, David, 2026-10-10)

Genuine emergence needs YES on all four:

- **LT1 (David's T1) Identity:** the squared quantity is the same object that does amplitude work elsewhere in the theory (it superposes, evolves linearly, interferes); if it appears only in the counting step, the square is a coincidence of form.
- **LT2 (David's T2) Prior inputs:** the law of R, the slice and the measure are fixed for independent reasons, ideally within a class named in advance, not tuned until |psi|^2 appears (the motivation clause; this is the contrast with Paper 1's trivial scheme).
- **LT3 (David's T3) Robustness:** the result survives refining the grain, changing the cut, and irrelevant further splitting.
- **LT4 (David's T4) Novel prediction:** it gives correct weights in a second, unrelated setup, or interference that was not put in.

**Scope and outcomes (David, 2026-10-10, about 11:39 ET, given directly to the Physics Lead in chat and relayed here; the exchange is not recorded in this repository; part of the L3 answer; this revises the earlier same-day reading, in which the test applied only once a Born-type result was claimed).** The test asks one question: was the Born rule put in by hand? **Failure** means circular reasoning that injects the Born rule directly, in whole or in part. The test can therefore fail before the full Born rule is claimed. It cannot be passed on a technicality, such as "we did not actually derive the Born rule, because our rule differs from it in some esoteric way". **Success** means no circularity has been detected. It does not prove emergence.

**Pre-emptive use (David, 2026-10-10, about 11:20 ET, revised about 11:39 ET, given directly to the Physics Lead in chat and relayed here; the exchange is not recorded in this repository; part of the L3 answer).** The test can also be run when there are only hints of a Born rule, such as the squared eigenvector of maximal-entropy random walks (MERW) (`notes/merw-litmus-scoping.md`). If a reviewer raises circularity, even prematurely, the record then has an answer ready. Run this way, the test can detect a partial injection (a failure). It cannot pass a claim that has not been made. For each LT, it records whether there is yet anything to check and what a later claim would have to show.

**Purpose (David, 2026-10-11, about 10:03 AM ET, given directly to the Physics Lead in chat and relayed here; the exchange is not recorded in this repository; part of the L3 answer)**

> It seems like we are tripping over our feet because we are trying to use one litmus test to answer two questions: first, have we successfully derived the Born rule in its entirety; second, are we guilty of cheating. We cannot use one binary litmus test to answer two binary questions. This litmus test is designed to detect cheating and that's it. It's possible to be innocent of cheating, which means we pass the test, and yet we still have not derived the full Born rule.

**Genuine emergence (David, 2026-10-11, about 10:09 AM ET, given directly to the Physics Lead in chat and relayed here; the exchange is not recorded in this repository; part of the L3 answer)**

> It remains true that genuine emergence needs a yes on all 4. A yes on all 4 is a necessary but not sufficient condition for genuine emergence. Genuine emergence requires two things: emergence (full derivation of the born rule), and that it be genuine (no cheating via circularity). This particular litmus test focuses on the word "genuine".

## Substitution test (David, 2026-10-10, about 11:39 ET, given directly to the Physics Lead in chat and relayed here; the exchange is not recorded in this repository; part of the L3 answer; proposed, not yet analysed formally)

Suppose the manuscript were rewritten with a different rule in place of the Born rule. Imagine, for example, that experimental theorists told the authors of the decision-theoretic (Deutsch–Wallace) derivation that experiment supports a different rule, and that the authors believed them. What else in the manuscript would need to change? Could the new rule simply be slid in place of the old one, with a footnote saying "this equation has been justified by experiment, so that's the one we are deriving now"?

If it could be slid in with nothing else changing, the rule was put in by hand, and the test fails. If a derivation is genuine, swapping the rule should break something upstream: an input, a lemma or a symmetry.

**David's conjecture (L3, unanalysed; recorded as stated; not a claim of this note or of v0.9):** the decision-theoretic derivation would fail this test, and this framework would pass it. Neither half is checked here. This note makes no comparison between the decision-theoretic programme and this framework, and v0.9 makes no Born-type claim for the test to be run on (v0.9 §2). How the substitution test relates to LT1–LT4 (for example, whether it sharpens LT2) is open.

## Outcome mapping (purpose and sorting principle settled by David's "Purpose" ruling; per-LT sort Lead's draft, pending David)

**Status.** Drafted from #263 (B1–B3, B5, B7) in the Physics Lead's voice, recorded in `reviews/born-litmus-merw-substitution-pressure-test.md`, and reworked to David's "Purpose" ruling above (relayed, not signed; #264 FD1). It is **not David's wording**. What the ruling decides is marked as his: the test's purpose and the sorting principle. Everything else here stays **Lead's draft, pending David**, including the per-LT sort of all four LTs. David's L3 answer above (the question, the working answer, "Scope and outcomes", "Pre-emptive use", "Purpose" and "Substitution test") is unchanged. David's "Genuine emergence" ruling above (relayed, not signed) settles part of how the working answer's "Genuine emergence needs YES on all four" reads: YES on all four is necessary but not sufficient for genuine emergence, which needs both a full derivation (emergence) and no cheating via circularity (genuine); this litmus test focuses on the second, and with the "Purpose" ruling it is designed to detect cheating only. Open for David, not decided here: whether YES on all four is also this test's pass condition, so that a NO on any LT is a failure, or whether some LTs (for example LT3) bear on emergence rather than on genuineness; this is the per-LT sort below.

- *Purpose (David's ruling).* The test answers one question only: was the Born rule put in by hand? A pass means no cheating has been detected. Whether the full Born rule has been derived is a separate question, which this test does not answer: as the quote above puts it, a pass is compatible with not having derived the full Born rule.
- *Sorting principle (from David's ruling).* A NO on an LT is either a **cheating signal**, which is a failure of this test, or a **completeness gap**, which is not a failure and not this test's question; a completeness gap only bears on the separate question of whether the full Born rule is derived.
- *Per-LT sort (Lead's draft, pending David).* Each mark applies David's principle to the LT's own wording; none is David's own sort.
  - **LT2 (Prior inputs): cheating signal (Lead's draft, pending David).** Its NO is an input "tuned until |psi|^2 appears", which is the Born rule put in by hand; its parenthetical names the contrast with Paper 1's trivial scheme, which the outcome analysis below finds fails only LT2.
  - **LT3 (Robustness): completeness gap (Lead's draft, pending David).** A result that does not survive refining the grain, changing the cut or further splitting has not derived the full rule, but such a NO can arise with no target weight in the inputs (the `notes/merw-litmus-scoping.md` R2 count below, unstable in L because of the tree's shape). A NO on LT3 whose source is a tuned input would show as a NO on LT2.
  - **LT1 (Identity): Lead's draft, pending David.** Either reading is open on David's words. A square that "appears only in the counting step" may be a square written into the count by hand (a cheating signal), or a square for which no amplitude-like object exists yet (a completeness gap; readiness v0.9 refresh, gap 6).
  - **LT4 (Novel prediction): Lead's draft, pending David.** Either reading is open on David's words. Failing to give correct weights in a second, unrelated setup reads as a completeness gap, while its "interference that was not put in" uses the test's own "put in" language and may read as a cheating signal.

- *Outcomes.* Before the "Purpose" ruling, the record did not say how a NO on each LT maps onto failure and success; this analysis (#263 B1) is kept as the reasoning behind the sort. As worded, LT2 is the test that asks about injection directly; its own parenthetical names the contrast with Paper 1's trivial scheme. That scheme (copies "proportional to |a_n|^2", Paper 1 §8; v0.9 §10), applied to the decoherent branches of a quantum state, would on its face meet LT1 (the squared quantity is the state's own amplitude), LT3 (squared moduli add under orthogonal refinement) and the first disjunct of LT4 (it returns the Born weights in every setup), and would fail only LT2. Conversely, a NO on LT1, LT3 or LT4 can have a source other than an input tuned to a target: for example, the count in `notes/merw-litmus-scoping.md` R2 is not stable in L because of the instance's tree shape, not because of any target weight. So "YES on all four" and "no circularity has been detected" are different bars, which David's "Purpose" ruling confirms: the test asks only the second, and a NO from a source other than an injection is a completeness gap, not a failure. Which LT outcomes return a failure follows the per-LT sort above, which is the Lead's draft, pending David.
- *LT2.* v0.9 §8.4 admits a motivation only if it selects the rule "within an independently named class of uniform rules", "or else states an empirical target". With the Born rule as that target, the second branch would admit what LT2 excludes ("not tuned until |psi|^2 appears"); in the other direction, LT2's "fixed for independent reasons", with a named class only "ideally", admits reasons §8.4 does not. v0.9 itself does not treat the clause as a circularity check: §8.4 leaves "weights forced, not assumed" as a later bar that would need "work on circularity". Read as a quantity inside the theory, LT2's "|psi|^2" needs an object that meets LT1, and v0.9 has none (readiness v0.9 refresh, gap 6); read as a target weight fixed outside the claim, as with v0.9 §10's tree "drawn by hand ... to match |a|^2", it needs no such object, and that is the form a pre-emptive run can check now.
- *LT3.* In v0.9 a prefix's per-vertex weight does not change with the cut, while the count's does (§9; C13, C14), and a later split of a descendant leaves per-vertex weights unchanged and changes counts (readiness §1.2, P7). So "changing the cut", and "irrelevant further splitting" read as later splitting, hold for (M1) by construction and fail for (M2), except for the cut clause where D is constant on the cuts compared (C13) or in the depth limit under asymptotic sibling balance (C15). The tilt is not one-way: a same-vertex split into arms with the same outcome moves both (readiness §1.2), and "refining the grain" can favour the count (re-describing a three-way decision as two binary ones moves the per-vertex weights from 1/3 each to 1/2, 1/4 and 1/4, while the count at the depth-2 cut, dead ends included (§9), stays at 1/3 each). "Irrelevant" is not defined: it needs an outcome labelling (readiness gap 5), and defined as "leaves the weights unchanged" it would presuppose a measure. The phrase is the branch-counting objection recorded in readiness §1.1, item 4; the clause parallels the Deutsch–Wallace fine-graining premise, which is disputed in the literature (readiness §1.1) (Dizadji-Bahmani 2015, *Brit. J. Phil. Sci.* 66(2), 257–283, doi:10.1093/bjps/axt035, argues "that Wallace is not successful in showing that BI [branching indifference] is rational" and that branch counting, which violates it, is a rational alternative; Kent 2010, in *Many Worlds?*, pp. 307–354, arXiv:0905.0624, argues that Wallace's axioms "are not constitutive of rationality" and that "Branching indifference, R5, is violated by Gell-Mann–Hartle aesthete strategies, among others"; Wallace 2010, §9, replies that branch counting "violates the combination of branching indifference and diachronic consistency"). This is recorded so that LT3 is not read as deciding (M1)/(M2). Under the per-LT sort above (Lead's draft, pending David), a NO on LT3 is a completeness gap, so this uneven bearing concerns completeness, not a cheating verdict.
- *Substitution condition.* Read literally, "swapping the rule should break something upstream" is met by every valid derivation. Its premises are inconsistent with any rival rule on the cases they cover, so something upstream always has to change. In Paper 1's trivial scheme (Paper 1 §8), what changes is the multiplicity assignment, which restates the rule. The verdict therefore turns on two judgements of each changed item: whether it is independently motivated, and whether it restates the rule on all or part of its domain (S3 in `notes/substitution-test-scoping.md`).
- *The conjecture's "pass".* How the substitution test relates to LT1–LT4 is open (options (R-a) onward in `notes/substitution-test-scoping.md`, none picked). If "Scope and outcomes" also governs this test, the conjecture's "pass" would mean only that no circularity was detected, not that the Born rule emerges; and since "Pre-emptive use" says the test "cannot pass a claim that has not been made", the conjecture's second half can concern only a future claim, as v0.9 makes none (§2).
