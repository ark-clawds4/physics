### S4: confirmed, as written (computed)

**Set-up.** At $\varepsilon=\Delta\ell(r_0)$ the inward path ends at $T=X=0$. The rule transports $u=\gamma(n+v\,e_\varphi)$ there, regular and future-directed (R1). The result is a germ on the bifurcation sphere:
- $E=-g(\xi,u)=0$, because $\xi=0$ there.
- $L=2\gamma v$.
- $\dot r=0$, and $r$ has a nondegenerate maximum, $r\approx2-\gamma^2\tau^2/8$.

It is radially stationary and a critical point of $K$ along its curvelet. It is not a turning point in #290's sense, which needs $r_0>2$. So Claude is right that the convention's formula ("$r'=2$") cannot express it.

**Its past.**
- Analytically: a past timelike curve from $T=X=0$ satisfies $F(\dot X^2-\dot T^2)<0$, so it enters $T<-|X|$ (region IV) at once and stays there. Inside, $\dot r^2=(2/r-1)(1+L^2/r^2)$, so $r$ decreases monotonically to the past.
- Pin (3) confirms this for five speeds: $r<2$ and $K>0.75$ throughout.

**The (a) test.** (a) as v0.10 defines it is $O'^{u'}\in F(O)\setminus\{\gamma_{O^u}(\tau):\tau\ge0\}$. It needs the target's past curvelet to pass through the source lump. Every past lump has $K>48/2^6=0.75>48/r_0^6$, so none is the source lump at $r_0>2$, so the edge fails (a). $K$ separates the lumps without any exterior label, so this holds on either gauge reading.
