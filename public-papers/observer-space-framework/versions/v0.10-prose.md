## Abstract

An *observer*, in this programme, is a physical object: the infinite jet of a real-analytic Lorentzian metric at a point, together with a future time direction. Observer space is the space of such objects. Under Continuous World Switching (CWS) the branch set is a singleton, and that null result is accepted. **Type (ii)** is a working, abandonable pad, the **Option E residue**: discrete graph joins between oriented observers, with analytic-geodesic segments in between. Type-(ii) edges exist as a working Postulate; no transition relation $R$ is specified. A history remembers whether it arrived at a vertex along a segment or by a jump, so the process is Markov on the pair (observer, arrival flag). Candidate branch times come from a lock-side slice along each geodesic, and the scalar that defines the slice is an open choice. **EPP1** is a named postulate: at a branch point, equal weight on each arm in force. Per-vertex equal weight is not Paper 1's count: at a named finite cut the two agree iff $\prod_{w<v}\deg(w)$ is constant on the cut. Per-vertex EPP1 is the working position, and David has ruled it the measure at L3 (§11, option (M1)); returning to Paper 1’s count at a named cut, option (M2), is the not-adopted alternative. No rule meets the motivation clause, so the bar actually in force is “uniform, not a hand list”. One uniform rule on a bound Schwarzschild orbit, the Kretschmann rule of §7, meets it; some of its edges fail the realisation condition (a) of §8.2, so as stated it is a model *without (a)*. Its restriction to (a)-admissible edges meets the bar too, but on that instance its branching is transient and its completed histories are countable (C33). This version does **not** derive the Born rule or Einstein's field equations. No law of $R$ is written.

## 1. Introduction

**The question.** Paper 1 (“The Observer as a Physical Object: Countability as the unique measure on observer space”, `papers/observer-space-ontology.md`) takes the elementary observer to be a local analytic object. Its countability thesis is that if distinct evolutions are countable, counting is the unique reasonable measure: “If the objects to be measured are distinct evolutions, and if those evolutions are countable”, then “Any other measure would have to weight some evolutions differently from others. That extra weighting would be a new piece of theory, not a reading of the space of objects” (Paper 1 §5). This paper asks how such an observer moves, how its evolution can fail to be unique when hitchhiking along a geodesic cannot branch, and what weights its branches then carry. Each answer is given with its status.

**Results and their status.** Each item names its status (legend in §3) and its claims (Appendix A).
1. *CWS singleton* (proposition, with sketch; §8.1; C28). Under CWS the branch set is a singleton: world switching changes lifetime, not path.
2. *Finite-$k$ matching* (result; §8.2; C27). Truncated-jet matching cannot create a branching edge: it is vacuous at $k=1$ between distinct lumps and prohibitive at $k\ge2$ for curvature-changing jumps.
3. *Continuum theorem* (result; §6.1; C24, C25). Under §4’s ensemble as written, marking vertex times where some compatible world ends marks a continuum; inextendibility alone does not repair this.
4. *The slice and its blind spots* (the slice scalar is a named extra; the empty-slice facts are a named constraint, not a law; §6.1; C18–C23, C32, C38). A lock-side slice $V^\tau$ gives discreteness per curvelet, in its open maximal domain. It gives no branch points on Minkowski, de Sitter or ESU for any lock-side scalar, nor on flat ΛCDM for $K$, $g^{ab}R_{ab}$ and $R_{ab}R^{ab}$, and every lock-side scalar is blind on locally symmetric lumps.
5. *The worked rule* (models and consistency witnesses, not adopted; §7, §8.2, §8.4; C16, C26, C30, C31, C33). The Kretschmann rule is stated, uniform and unmotivated, and as stated it is a model without (a); there its completed histories are uncountable (proof, C16). Its (a)-restriction is a stated, uniform, lock-side model whose branching on the instance is transient, with countable completed histories.
6. *The cut criterion and its limit* (results; the criterion and the limit "iff" with proof; §9; C12–C15). At a named finite cut, per-vertex weighting and Paper 1’s count agree iff $D$ is constant on the cut. With finite stars, depth-$n$ counts converge to per-vertex weights on every cylinder set iff siblings are asymptotically balanced.
7. *No jump dominance* (a named constraint, not a law; §9; C17, C33, C34). If typical histories are to show geodesic motion over an observed window, any future $R$ must meet (J1) or (J2). The worked rule as stated fails both; its (a)-restriction meets a late-window (J1) and fails (J2).
8. *The Zeno-mass convention* (Open; §8.3; C35). Three readings give different weights, and none is picked.

**Plan.** §2 collects what the paper does not claim, and §3 gives the status legend and the notation. §4–§6 set out the object, the two motion types and the slice. §7 defines the worked example. §8 gives motion and the gates, §9 the measure, and §10 Bell and Obs-locality. §11 lists open problems, hopes and open calls, and §12 relates the paper to Paper 1 and its neighbours. Appendix A pins the claims; Appendix B gives the pedigree of the two measure rules.

**The object.** An *observer*, in this programme, is a physical object: the infinite jet of a real-analytic Lorentzian metric at a point, together with a future time direction. Equivalently, it is the simply-connected analytic patch $P(O)$ obtained by local continuation of that jet, carrying that direction. Here the direction is read as a future unit timelike vector $u$, taken modulo the isometries of the germ that fix the point (§4). The direction is always part of the object, not something added only when branching is discussed. Observer space is the space of such objects. Nothing reconstructs a whole world from one of them. (Labels C1–C38 refer to the claims of Appendix A, each checked against a named instance.)

**Relation to Paper 1.** Paper 1’s object is kept unchanged; its motion is revised (histories are now discontinuous, and their jumps carry no proper time); and its measure is revised (a per-vertex equal-weight postulate, EPP1, replaces the count, so Paper 1’s thesis that countability makes counting the unique measure is not carried). This is the working position, and David has ruled it the measure at L3 (§11, option (M1)); returning to Paper 1’s count at a named cut, option (M2), is the not-adopted alternative.

Motion has two layers. **Type (i)** is geodesic hitchhiking. Under Continuous World Switching (CWS) the branch set is a singleton, and that null result is accepted. **Type (ii)** is a working, abandonable pad, the **Option E residue**: discrete graph joins between oriented observers, with analytic-geodesic segments in between. The branch set at a vertex is
$$
B_R(O^u)=\{[\gamma_{O^u}]\}\cup\mathrm{succ}_R(O^u),
$$
and the arms are its elements. A history remembers whether it arrived at a vertex along a segment or by a jump, so the process is Markov on the pair (observer, arrival flag). Candidate branch times come from a lock-side slice along each geodesic, and the scalar that defines the slice is an open choice. A public controlled example must be given by a rule that is stated, uniform and motivated. No rule meets the motivation clause, so the bar actually in force is “uniform, not a hand list”. One uniform rule on a bound Schwarzschild orbit, the Kretschmann rule of §7, meets it; some of its edges fail the realisation condition (a) of §8.2, so as stated it is a model *without (a)*. Its restriction to (a)-admissible edges meets the bar too, but on that instance its branching is transient and its completed histories are countable (C33).

**EPP1** is a named postulate: at a branch point, equal weight on each arm in force. Its pedigree is the per-split rule, applied in MMWI (2006) and stated as SHPMP 2008’s Definition 3 (Appendix B). Its domain is not determined, because no transition relation $R$ is adopted; the unadopted worked model has domain states (C11, C29). **EPP2**, weight by ensemble size, is kept as a foil.

This paper exhibits **no** no-no-go sketch. §10 lists what one would need. Compatibility of Obs-local rules with Bell’s theorem (Bell 1964) is a Hope (§11). The Born rule and Einstein’s equations remain later goals, not theorems.

## 2. Firewall: what this paper does not claim

**Firewall.** This version does **not** derive the Born rule or Einstein's field equations. It does not treat $|B|\ge2$ as achieved under CWS.

It does not count a hand-listed digraph as a controlled public example. The Kretschmann $\pm\varepsilon\hat n$ rule is stated and uniform, so it meets the bar in force. But it is unmotivated, so it fails the motivated gate. As stated it models the schema without realisation (a); its (a)-restriction is a model too. Neither is a controlled public example, and neither is an adopted $R$. "(a) has a model" does not mean "(a) has an ongoing branching model": under (a) on the instance, branching is transient (C33). The throat convention of §7 is part of the unadopted example's definition, not a law.

It makes no Born claim from path counting or from the maximal-entropy random walk; MERW's $\psi^2$ density is a named hazard for (M2), not a route taken; what a Born-type claim through it would have to show is scoped in `notes/merw-litmus-scoping.md`. It does not choose arm multiplicities $N$ to reproduce $|a|^2$, it puts no $1/N$ in by hand, and it does not treat $1/|B_R|$ as implied by bare $R$.

It does not claim Bell is refuted. It does not frame the problem as Everett versus Deutsch–Wallace; the §12 row is a firewall only. It is not a local hidden-variable theory that beats Bell. It is not Stoica, not Gielen–Wise and not Larsson.

The leftover inventory of formal properties of $R$ is not restarted: no acyclicity or descendant-finiteness programme. No law of $R$ is written. No grain of $\mathrm{Obs}$ or $V$ is locked.

Finite-$k$ truncated-jet matching cannot create a branching edge. At $k=1$ it constrains nothing between distinct lumps; at $k\ge2$ it only forbids. Under §4’s ensemble as written, extend-versus-not vertex selection is not discrete. Discrete branch points need a named extra (a structure not supplied by the locked ontology, which must therefore be declared by name): a lock-side $\tau$-slice or a chosen sub-ensemble.

Per-vertex equal weight is not Paper 1's count: at a named finite cut the two agree iff $\prod_{w<v}\deg(w)$ is constant on the cut. Per-vertex EPP1 does not carry Paper 1's uniqueness-from-countability thesis. No-jump-dominance (J1/J2) and the empty-slice facts are named constraints, not laws. Type (ii) is abandonable. The measure rules are named only EPP1 and EPP2. Figures from random samples are marked "sampled".

**Does not claim.** This paper does not claim any of the following:
- the Born rule, or Einstein’s equations;
- $|B|\ge2$ under CWS;
- a controlled example clearing the motivated gate, or a written law of $R$;
- a locked grain of $\mathrm{Obs}$ or $V$;
- multiplicities tuned to $|a|^2$, or $1/|B_R|$ implied by bare $R$;
- that Bell is refuted;
- an Everett-versus-Deutsch–Wallace frame; Stoica, Gielen–Wise or Larsson;
- the leftover inventory;
- a whole world reconstructed from $O$;
- discrete vertices from ensemble-timed (extend-versus-not) selection under §4’s ensemble as written;
- finite-$k$ matching as the escape from the CWS singleton;
- per-history discreteness from $V^\tau$ alone;
- a no-no-go sketch;
- Paper 1’s uniqueness thesis under EPP1;
- an ongoing branching model under (a).

## 4. Ontology

The definitions in this section are the working ones for review. The informal notes in `mathematical-framework.md` yield where they disagree.

**Metric germ.** Write $O=j_p^\infty g$ for the infinite jet of a real-analytic Lorentzian metric $g$ at $p$. Equivalently, $O$ is presented as the simply-connected analytic patch $P(O)$ obtained by analytic continuation of that jet. This is a germ-level identification of presentations, not a claim that a maximal global manifold is unique. Smooth is not enough; analyticity is required.

**Lump identity.** Two presentations that differ by a pointed analytic diffeomorphism renaming the same local patch are the same *lump*. Gauge is this identity.

**Oriented observer.** $O^u$ is the class of triples (presentation, $p$, $u$), with $u$ a future-directed unit timelike vector at $p$, under pointed analytic diffeomorphisms that carry $u$. In short, **$u$ is taken modulo the isotropy of the germ**. Null observers are excluded. The quotient is not optional. A lump has no canonical tangent space, so without the quotient the successor sets, branch sets and EPP1 weights below would not be functions of physical data. For the flat lump the isotropy is the Lorentz group, and the whole hyperboloid of unit timelike vectors is one oriented observer: a branch set that would count three arms without the quotient counts two with it (C9).

**Vertex space.** $V$ is the space of oriented observers $O^u$. It *is* Paper 1’s observer space, with Paper 1’s “time direction” read as $u$. That is a reading, stated as such. Paper 1 distinguishes its observer from Gielen and Wise's (2013) observer space, whose points are unit future timelike vectors (a 7-dimensional space, which they also define abstractly, with no spacetime assumed). Here $u$ sits on a lump, and $V$ is not their 7-manifold. $\mathrm{Obs}$ denotes the space of lumps without $u$ and is used only where orientation plays no role; the one exception is the name *Obs-local* (§10), which is a condition on oriented states $(O^u,a)$. It is not Larsson’s (2007) Quantum Jet Theory either.

**Spacetime, and the cost of analyticity.** A spacetime $M$ is a connected real-analytic Lorentzian 4-manifold; Einstein’s equation is not assumed. The cost of analyticity has two halves. Mathematically, two real-analytic metrics on one connected analytic $M$ with the same infinite jet at a point coincide on all of $M$ (identity theorem); the jet does not determine $M$ itself (C24, C25). That rigidity is why type-(i) hitchhiking yields one branch and why type (ii) has to be extra structure. Physically, there are no localised geometric degrees of freedom of the smooth kind, generic smooth metrics (including generic smooth solutions of general relativity) are excluded, and matter fields are open, as in Paper 1.

**Measurement, informally (not ontology).** Pictures of how a local geometric change might register in a germ are optional and are not part of the locked object; none is used here. Matter fields are open, as in Paper 1.

**Worlds and relative ensembles.** A world is $\mathcal W=(M,p)$. $E_{\mathcal W}(O)$ is the set of pointed worlds whose point $p$ is an occurrence of the germ $O$, and $E_M(O)$ is the corresponding collection of manifolds. Indexing by $O^u$ gives the same pointed worlds, since each occurrence of the lump carries an occurrence of $O^u$; orientation enters only through the observer's curvelet $\gamma_{O^u}$ (defined in §5; used for the slice in §6.1). These are relative ensembles: occurrences of the same lump, not extra vertices, and not a second observer. Nothing reconstructs a whole world from $O$. Sibling points in one $M$ with the same germ are multiplicity, not a fork. By the continuum theorem of §6.1, $E_{\mathcal W}(O)$ is at least a continuum. Bertrand’s problem (on a continuum, "equal weight" does not by itself fix a measure, so ratios depend on a chosen parametrisation) concerns ratios on it, not its cardinality.

**Identity versus timing.** The ensemble does not vote on the identity of the elementary object; that Paper 1 claim stays. Nor does it select *when* branching may occur. Choosing vertex times by which ensemble members die would be a timing vote. This paper calls any load-bearing use of a particular ensemble member E-smuggling: a timing vote, or (§10) the choice of one world in which to embed two observers. It is not used. Whether $E_{\mathcal W}$ should be restricted to inextendible or maximal members is open. Inextendibility alone does not restore discrete death times (C25).

**Lock-side.** A property is *lock-side* if it is fixed by the oriented germ $O^u$ alone: by jet data and $u$, and hence also by the curvelet $\gamma_{O^u}$ that $O^u$ determines. It makes no reference to ensemble members or to history data such as an origin of $\tau$.

**Compatibility (sketch).** $O'^{u'}$ is an allowable future of $O^u$ if some connected analytic world contains occurrences $p$ and $p'$ joined by a future-directed timelike geodesic from $p$ to $p'$. The relation is not assumed transitive. If kept, it *is* realisation (a) of §8.2, with the same endpoint and arrival convention, and so it is lock-side.

## 5. Type (i) versus type (ii)

**Type (i)** is defined by its path. A type-(i) segment is one whose path in $V$ is the *curvelet* $\gamma_{O^u}$: the unique curve in $V$ traced by the geodesic from $(p,u)$ in the patch $P(O)$ (Prop. 3.2 and Thm 3 of `papers/geodesic-lifts.md`). Geodesic lifts in any world agree with it on their common domain (Cor. 3.1 there); only their lifetimes differ. That is why CWS, which switches worlds, is still a type-(i) package. Analytic lifts do not fork after a delay.

**Type (ii)** pairs are those that are not successive oriented germs along $\gamma_{O^u}$. At a type-(ii) jump a history is **discontinuous in oriented observer space**. The lump path in $\mathrm{Obs}$ may stay constant. In the Einstein static universe (ESU), a bare boost of $u$ at a fixed event is type (ii) even after the isotropy quotient, while the lump never changes (C10). This is a classification example only: in ESU the slice of §6.1 is empty, so such a pair is never an edge.

**Oriented split.** $(O^u,O'^{u'})$ is type (i) iff $O'^{u'}=\gamma_{O^u}(\tau)$ for some $\tau>0$ in the curvelet’s maximal analytic domain. A world-dependent lifetime is not used, because punching holes makes lifetimes arbitrary. $\tau=0$ is the identity and is not a pair. Pairs reachable only at $\tau<0$ are backward, not future continuations. Whether $R$ may contain them is not decided: no pinned instance needs them, and realisation (a) does not by itself exclude them, since (a) asks only for *some* future-directed realisation. Every other pair is type (ii), including bare orientation changes at a fixed lump. These are ordinary type-(ii) candidates with no separate selection rule.

**Not restored.** The 2007 tree built by superimposing geodesic lifts is not a theorem of hitchhiking. **Not restarted.** The leftover inventory of formal properties of $R$ is not imported.

**Terminology.** “Segment” means a type-(i) piece; “edge” means an element of $R$, and nothing else.

## 6. Vertices: the slice $V^\tau$, and dying is not an arm

### 6.1 A candidate lock-side slice $V^\tau$

**Continuum theorem.** Under §4’s ensemble as written, with no inextendibility requirement, marking vertex times where some compatible world ends marks a continuum. For every $\tau_0>0$ in the domain of the observer’s geodesic in a compatible world, removing the point the geodesic reaches at $\tau_0$ (if it has not passed that point before) leaves a compatible world in which the observer’s geodesic ends at $\tau_0$; C24 is the Minkowski case. Inextendibility alone does not repair this. In flat space with a conical defect the ending times of geodesics aimed at the axis form a continuum, since the axis distance is an isometry invariant (C25). Ensemble-timed vertex selection is therefore demoted to an in-house schematic, or else it requires an explicitly chosen discrete sub-ensemble, which is a grain on $E_{\mathcal W}$.

**Candidate engine.** $V^\tau$ is the set of oriented germs $O^u$ at which $\tau\mapsto s(\gamma_{O^u}(\tau))$ has an isolated critical point at $\tau=0$. Alternatively, it is where $s\circ\gamma$ crosses a named *level* $s_0$. Here $s$ is a lock-side scalar with $s\circ\gamma$ non-constant. Membership is lock-side, since $u^a\nabla_a s$ is jet data. One-variable analyticity gives discreteness per curvelet, in its open maximal domain. Accumulation at a singular endpoint remains possible (C8). The no-Zeno clause handles that, not $V^\tau$.

**What the slice can and cannot see.**
- *Scalar class.* The class meant is lock-side scalars on oriented germs: analytic functions of the curvature, its covariant derivatives and $u$. Examples are $K$ (the Kretschmann scalar), the Ricci scalar $g^{ab}R_{ab}$ (not the transition relation $R$), $R_{ab}R^{ab}$, $R_{ab}u^au^b$, and the Bel–Robinson superenergy $W=T_{abcd}u^au^bu^cu^d$, which in vacuum equals $E_{ab}E^{ab}+B_{ab}B^{ab}$, with $E_{ab}$ and $B_{ab}$ the electric and magnetic parts of the Weyl tensor relative to $u$ (this equality fixes the normalisation of $T_{abcd}$).
  - Polynomial invariants that do not involve $u$ are a subclass, and they are blind on VSI lumps, where all polynomial invariants vanish although the curvature does not ($R_{uxux}=-e^{-u^2}$ in MTW's sign convention and the null coordinates of C22, where $u$ is a coordinate, not the 4-velocity). On that vacuum plane wave with a Gaussian profile, $W$ has one vertex, at the wave peak.
  - Which member of the class, and critical point versus level, is a named extra. It is an honest grain-like choice on the curvelet, not a theorem that $V$ or $\mathrm{Obs}$ is discrete, and not a vote of $E_{\mathcal W}$. The worked examples use $K$.
- *Blind spot of the whole class.* On **locally symmetric lumps** ($\nabla\mathrm{Riem}=0$), local isometries (transvections) carry each point and tangent of a geodesic to every later one. The oriented germ is therefore constant along every curvelet, and every lock-side scalar is constant. This covers Minkowski, de Sitter, ESU and the constant-profile (Cahen–Wallach) plane wave, where $V^\tau$ is empty although $W>0$ (C32). No choice within the class fixes this.
- *Codimension one.* $V^\tau$ is not a discrete set of oriented germs. It is the codimension-one zero set of $u^a\nabla_a s$ on germs whose curvelet has $s\circ\gamma$ non-constant (so it omits C21's circular locus), and it is discrete only along each curvelet. On the Schwarzschild exterior it is a two-parameter family (C21).
- *Special orbits.* On Schwarzschild, circular orbits have $K\circ\gamma$ constant and no vertex. Bound radial geodesics ($E<1$) have exactly one vertex, at the apex, and infall with $E\ge1$ has none (C20).
- *The choice matters.* In flat ΛCDM, $(R_{ab}u^au^b)^2$ puts a vertex for comoving observers at the onset of acceleration ($z\approx0.671$), where $K$ puts none (C23).
- *No time tags.* Tagging vertices with (lump, $\tau$) is not lock-side, because $\tau$ needs an origin, which is history data. Tags could separate recurrences but cannot select times where the slice selects none. They are not used.

**Named constraint, not a law: the slice is empty on standard backgrounds.** The example slice gives **no branch points** on Minkowski, de Sitter or ESU, for every lock-side scalar, because there the oriented germ is constant along every curvelet (C19). It gives none on flat ΛCDM for $K$, $g^{ab}R_{ab}$ and $R_{ab}R^{ab}$, which are strictly monotone, with nowhere-vanishing derivative, along every timelike geodesic (C18). Under this engine the flat laboratory never branches. No flat-space branching is claimed, so this is not an overclaim, but it is recorded as a cost. Under this engine, the retired toy’s flat root cannot be a branch point either. For a **freely falling** near-flat laboratory in a Schwarzschild exterior, the $K$-vertices are exactly its radial turning points ($dK/d\tau=-288M^2u^r/r^7$, C38), so under the example slice its branch opportunities are fixed by its own free fall, not by anything an experiment does. A supported laboratory is not a geodesic observer and lies outside the model while matter is open.

### 6.2 Dying is not an arm

When an ensemble member’s world ends, that is ensemble dropout, not a second arm, unless a named sink is added. Several analytic extenders forced to the same next germ count as the single $[\gamma]$ arm. So $|B|\ge2$ needs genuine type-(ii) edges in $R$. By convention, a history that ends and one that continues through the same oriented germs count as one evolution. This follows Paper 1’s first-disagreement cut together with the rule that dying is not an arm.

## 8. Motion: the CWS baseline, the Option E residue, and the gates

### 8.1 Type (i) baseline: the CWS singleton

Under CWS, allowed paths are piecewise geodesic lifts. The traveller may switch worlds at a shared oriented germ, the path in $V$ is $C^\infty$, and orientation does not jump.

**Proposition (CWS singleton).** Under CWS, and under either equivalence on path germs — (E1) equal infinite-order path-jet at the start, or (E2) agreement on a common initial interval — $|B_{\mathrm{CWS}}(O^u)|=1$. EPP1 has empty domain under CWS alone.

*Sketch.*
1. Every CWS piece that starts at $O^u$ is a geodesic lift through the shared oriented germ.
2. The geodesic with initial data $(p,u)$ is unique, and its germ path is $\gamma_{O^u}$ in any world (Prop. 3.2 of `papers/geodesic-lifts.md`).
3. So all allowed paths agree on an initial interval (E2), hence in infinite-order jet (E1), and there is one class.

World switching changes lifetime, not path. In Minkowski space the flat lump modulo isotropy is a single oriented germ, its curvelet is constant, and there is one class (C28).

CWS is therefore not the branching law. The singleton comes from the shared germ, the uniqueness of the geodesic piece and equivalence at the start. So the way out of it is not to “reject infinite-order matching”.

### 8.2 Option E residue: the working branching pad

On each segment the motion is an analytic-geodesic lift. (Worked example: the *Kretschmann rule* and *the instance*, both defined in §7.) Joins are discontinuous type-(ii) jumps plus incidence in a graph. **The arms at a vertex are the elements of $B_R$**, not the out-star $\mathrm{succ}_R$ alone. If the continue arm were left out of the arm set while each edge kept the weight $1/(1+|\mathrm{succ}_R|)$, the weights would sum to only $|\mathrm{succ}_R|/(1+|\mathrm{succ}_R|)$.

**Truncated-jet matching is not a branching mechanism.** Matching jets to a fixed finite order $k$ is automatic on the continue arm. It is vacuous at $k=1$ between distinct lumps, since in $u$-adapted normal coordinates every 1-jet is the same. At $k\ge2$ it is prohibitive for curvature-changing jumps, since the 2-jet is the Riemann tensor in the $u$-frame (C27). It cannot create a branching edge, and pairing it with graph joins adds no branching power.

**Working Postulate: $R\ne\emptyset$.** Type-(ii) edges exist; no $R$ is specified. The Postulate comes with three clauses.
- **Not derivable.** Locked data alone determine no type-(ii) edge: hitchhiking gives only pairs along the curvelet, and any other pair needs a further rule (Prop. 13, `papers/type-ii-adopted.md`).
- **Existence only.** It asserts $|B_R|\ge2$ at no named vertex and does not by itself give EPP1 a domain. Existence is asserted; no $R$ is adopted.
- **Weakest form.** It is Paper 1’s non-uniqueness premise in its weakest, existential form. $R\ne\emptyset$ ranges over all of $V$, whereas “the evolution is not unique” along given histories would need $\mathrm{succ}_R\ne\emptyset$ along them.

$R=\emptyset$ is CWS. Dropping the package leaves layer (i) unchanged, and it makes Paper 1’s “the evolution is not unique” false.

**Composition.**
1. $R\subseteq V^\tau\times V$, where $V^\tau$ is the slice of §6.1. This is a declared constraint on $R$’s *source* set: it fixes when branching may happen, never the targets. It inherits named-extra status from the choice of slice scalar. Without it, every point of a curvelet would be a potential attachment point, a continuum.
2. **Strict alternation.** After a type-(ii) jump, a segment of positive proper time is traversed before the next branch decision (a *decision* is a visit of a history to a branch point at which more than one arm is in force). Proper time is carried only along segments. A history is therefore no longer Paper 1’s curve parametrised by proper time. It is a sequence of segments in $V$ separated by jumps that carry no $\tau$.
3. $R$ contains no type-(i) pairs. Out-degree means type-(ii) out-degree.
4. $\mathrm{succ}_R(O^u):=\{O'^{u'}:(O^u,O'^{u'})\in R\}$.
5. **First-edge equivalence.** Two continuations from $O^u$ are equivalent iff both open with the same edge of $R$, or both open with a positive-length segment along $\gamma_{O^u}$, whatever they do later. At the $r=10$ periastron of the Kretschmann rule (§7) this gives 3 classes (C11).
6. $B_R(O^u):=\{[\gamma_{O^u}]\}\cup\mathrm{succ}_R(O^u)$, so $|B_R|=1+|\mathrm{succ}_R|$ always, since every oriented germ has a forward geodesic segment. The continue arm is the $[\gamma]$ term, not an edge of $R$. As a definition, $B_R$ is a property of the pair $(O^u,R)$.
7. **Arrival flag.** The state is $(O^u,a)$, where $a\in\{\mathrm{seg},\mathrm{jump}\}$ records how $O^u$ was reached. The arm set in force is
$$A(O^u,\mathrm{seg})=B_R(O^u),\qquad A(O^u,\mathrm{jump})=\{[\gamma_{O^u}]\}.$$
The root has $a=\mathrm{seg}$ by convention. EPP1’s kernel is thus a property of $(O^u,a,R)$, and the process is Markov on $(O^u,a)$, **not** on $O^u$ alone. The same oriented germ has 1 arm when reached by a jump and $\ge2$ when revisited along a segment (C6). That enlarged state is the price of alternation, and it is stated here rather than hidden.
8. **No-Zeno (named clause).** Along each history, branch points are finite in every bounded interval of accumulated proper time. A history whose branch points accumulate at finite accumulated $\tau$, including at a singular endpoint of one segment, **ends there** and is outside EPP1’s domain. The range of clause (2) of the hard gate, and the open convention for the EPP1 mass of such histories, are stated in §8.3.

Without some arrival rule, clause (2) of the hard gate fails per history. On the Kretschmann rule, jump targets are themselves vertices, so one history can take infinitely many branch points at a single value of accumulated $\tau$ (C5). Two alternatives to strict alternation are rejected:
- **(B)**, consecutive jumps counted with multiplicity under a bound $N$. On the instance, chains of edges are infinite, so $N$ would be a law-like number imposed on histories, and making it a property of $R$ would restart the leftover inventory.
- **(C)**, target avoidance, $R\subseteq V^\tau\times(V\setminus V^\tau)$. This would be Markov on $O^u$ alone, but it excludes the only worked instance, whose targets are all turning points.

Because the kernel under (A) acts at each visit, **walk semantics follows from option (A)**: EPP1 applies per visit, and no separate direction postulate on $R$ is needed. None could make histories acyclic here anyway, since successive periastra of a bound Schwarzschild orbit are one oriented germ.

**Spacetime realisation: two options, not interchangeable.**

- **(a) Realised edges.** Every edge $(O^u,O'^{u'})$ is realised: some connected real-analytic world contains occurrences $p$ of $O$ and $p'$ of $O'$ joined by a future-directed timelike geodesic from $p$ to $p'$.
  - *Endpoint convention.* The realising geodesic leaves $p$ along some $u''$, necessarily $u''\ne u$ modulo the isotropy of the germ. Leaving along $u$ would make it $\gamma_{O^u}$ and the pair type (i), so $R$ would be empty (C3). It arrives at $p'$ with tangent $u'$: the target orientation is the arrival tangent.
  - *Factorisation.* Every (a)-edge is therefore a bare change of orientation at $O$ followed by type-(i) transport.
  - *Lock-side form.* Because the geodesic arriving with tangent $u'$ is unique, (a) is lock-side and world-free:
    $$(O^u,O'^{u'})\ \text{is (a)-admissible}\iff O'^{u'}\in F(O)\setminus\{\gamma_{O^u}(\tau):\tau\ge0\},\qquad F(O):=\{\gamma_{(O,u'')}(\tau):u''\ \text{future unit},\ \tau>0\}.$$
    In words, the target’s own curvelet passes through the source lump in its past.
  - *The worked example under (a).* At the $r=10$ periastron, the Kretschmann rule’s outward edges fail (a): their target orbits, $[10.108,21.332]$ ($c=0.01$) and $[11.081,39.003]$ ($c=0.1$), never return to $r=10$ (C30). Its inward edges there pass: $[8.9332,10.1029]$ ($c=0.1$) and $[9.8926,18.7504]$ ($c=0.01$) do return (C31). The inward chain at $c=0.01$ stays admissible for 12 steps and fails at step 13 (target $8.7383$; C31). So **the Kretschmann example models the schema without (a)**; imposing (a) would drop, on the instance, the outward edge at the periastron and the inward edge at the apastron (§7). At the germ $X'$ of C6, the rule as stated gives 3 arms after a segment, and **(a) leaves 2**, since the outward edge there fails. The arrival dependence survives either way.
  - *No proper time.* The realising geodesic labels admissibility and carries none of the history’s proper time.
  - *What (a) forbids and permits.* By analytic rigidity, $O'$ lies in the analytic-continuation class of $O$, up to monodromy. (This is the class, not “the maximal analytic world”, which need not be unique.) (a) **forbids every edge whose target lies off $F(O)$**. That includes all cross-continuation jumps, the ones a measurement picture would want (by analytic rigidity, outcome geometries that differ locally lie in different continuation classes): flat to FLRW, the edge of the retired toy (`papers/B-ge2-minimal-toy.md`), and Schwarzschild $M=1$ to $M=2$, whose germs are separated by the invariants $K$ and $|\nabla K|^2$ (C1). It also includes same-class targets whose past curvelet misses $O$, such as the Kretschmann outward edges at the periastron (C30). It **permits curvature-changing jumps within one class**, for example Schwarzschild $M=1$ from a periastron at $r=10$ to a radial germ at $r=8$, where $K$ rises by a factor of about 3.8 (C2). The cost of (a) is real, but it is not “no curvature change”.
  - *In-patch continuum (soft Prop. 13).* The (a)-admissible targets of one $O^u$ form a continuum within the class (C4). (a) is a necessary condition, not a law, and it selects no countable star.
- **(b) No realisation required.** (b) admits cross-continuation edges **and** same-class edges that fail (a); not-(a) is not the same as cross-continuation. Any edge between distinct continuation classes, and any edge whose target lies outside $F(O)$ (such as the Kretschmann outward edges at the periastron), has taken (b).

Compatibility (§4), if kept, is (a). A second route to (a)-realising the Kretschmann outward target at the periastron, by static recurrence of its lump at later Killing times, works at the level of lumps but **fails at oriented level** under the arrival convention (C30). It is not used.

**The (a)-restricted model.** Restricting the Kretschmann rule of §7 to its (a)-admissible edges gives a rule that is stated, uniform and lock-side, since the filter is the lock-side $F(O)$ test. It has $|B_R|=2$ at both turning points of the orbit $[10,20]$, for $c=0.1$ and for $c=0.01$. So “not fatal” (the HOLE block, §8.4) does not depend on dropping (a). But on the instance its branching is **transient** (C33):
- At $c=0.1$, no orbit reached by a jump has an (a)-admissible edge, so a history jumps once, almost surely. At $c=0.01$, jump chains end within 14 jumps; under EPP1 a history jumps 12, 13 or 14 times, with probabilities $26/3^{12}\approx4.9\times10^{-5}$, $9338/3^{10}\approx0.158$ and $447373/3^{12}\approx0.842$ (exact, C33).
- At every turning point $m\le1$ (one outgoing edge at most), so each decision is a fair coin between continuing and the one jump, and the pure-geodesic history has weight $2^{-n}$ after $n$ decisions.
- Decisions are almost surely finite, and completed histories are countable.

In this (a)-restricted model on the instance, histories almost surely jump, and almost surely they stop after finitely many decisions. This paragraph states what the model does. It adopts no $R$.

### 8.3 The hard gate, per history

A **branch point** is a state $(O^u,a)$ on a history with $|A(O^u,a)|\ge2$. EPP1 applies at a branch point where the hard gate holds, that is, when both of the following hold:

1. $A(O^u,a)$ is **finite**, or countable with a named exhaustion declared as extra; **and**
2. **along each history, there are finitely many branch points in every bounded interval of accumulated proper time.**

Clause (2) is supplied in two parts. Per segment, $V^\tau$ supplies it: branch points are finite on every compact proper-time interval of each segment, within its open maximal domain. Per history, it is supplied by strict alternation plus the no-Zeno clause (§8.2, items 2 and 8). It is **not** a consequence of $V^\tau$ alone. No-Zeno also excludes accumulation at a singular endpoint: the history ends there.

On the bound Schwarzschild instance no accumulation arises. After any vertex (reached by jump or by segment), the next vertex on a bound orbit is at least about 111.59 units of proper time away, so a history makes at most $1+T/111.59$ decisions up to accumulated time $T$ (C7). In general accumulation can arise: a flat FLRW model with an oscillating Hubble rate has infinitely many critical points of $K$ before a finite singular time (C8).

**Range of clause (2).** It ranges over the histories through the state, from the state onward. Whether Zeno histories are first excised by item 8 of §8.2 is the open convention below: including them gives reading (Z-i); excising them with the mass kept gives (Z-ii); excising and conditioning gives (Z-iii).

**Zeno-mass convention (open).** Item 8 excises Zeno histories. Whether their EPP1 mass is kept on the ended histories or conditioned away is not settled. The three readings are (Z-i) a state-level gate over all histories through the state, (Z-ii) history-level excision with the mass kept on the ended histories, and (Z-iii) the Zeno mass conditioned away. They give different weights. Take a root with arms $X$, ordinary thereafter, and $Y$; below $Y$, vertices at accumulated $\tau=1-2^{-k}$ each carry 2 arms, so the Zeno set has mass $1/2$ under the per-vertex product with the gate ignored. Then $X$'s weight is undefined under (Z-i), $1/2$ under (Z-ii) and $1$ under (Z-iii) (C35). This paper does not pick one, because the choice moves weights; it is listed in §11.

### 8.4 The digraph fact and the public gate

The in-house note `papers/B-ge2-minimal-toy.md` is retired as a demonstration of discrete vertices. At most it records a digraph fact: some abstract $(V,R)$ has $|B_R|=2$ under the composition above. That example is hand-built in its ensemble, its vertex and its out-edge.

**Public controlled-example gate.** An $R$ qualifies only if it is given by a **stated** rule on lumps that is **uniform** (one rule in germ invariants at every vertex, with no finite case list) and **motivated**. The arm count must be a consequence of the rule, and clause (2) must hold per history.

**Motivation test.** Covariance, naturality and isometry-equivariance hold for *every* rule defined on lumps, because lumps are diffeomorphism classes. So they do not count as motivation. A motivation qualifies only if it selects the rule, up to named constants, within an independently named class of uniform rules, or else states an empirical target.

**The Kretschmann rule (§7) under the gate.**
- It is **stated, uniform and unmotivated** under the test (C26). It meets the bar in force but fails the motivated gate.
- It is a consistency witness for the schema **without (a)**. On the instance one edge at each turning point fails (a), the outward edge at the periastron and the inward edge at the apastron (§8.2; the apastron targets are in §7), so as stated it is a (b) model, not a controlled public example. Its (a)-restriction is a model too, with transient branching (§8.2, C33).

**Which branch of the gate holds now.** No rule meets the motivation clause, so the bar in force is “uniform, not a hand list”. The Kretschmann rule as stated is a (b) model, and it meets that bar. Its (a)-restriction also meets it, with transient branching (C33). Neither is motivated. The relocated problem ends at Bertrand’s problem over admissible (slice, rule) pairs, because the slice scalar and level are a second free choice.

“Weights forced, not assumed” remains a later bar. It would need a law of $R$, a typicality rule, and work on circularity. It is not claimed here.

**State of the relocated HOLE (from #105).** The HOLE is the CWS-singleton finding of §8.1, relocated by #105. Its state:
- cleared as a definition ($B_R$ given $R$);
- **still relocated as a problem**, with a final address: the law of $R$, its motivation, and the choice of $\tau$-slice;
- not fatal: the Option E residue is consistent, and the Kretschmann rule on a bound Schwarzschild orbit is a model of it without realisation (a). Its (a)-restriction is a model too, but on the instance its branching is **transient** and completed histories are countable (C33).

### 8.5 The cost of $R$: the hidden-variable smell, kept loud

**Soft Prop. 13 (a remark, not a separate proposition).** No law of $R$ is written. The lock-side candidates examined in-house (for example `papers/law-of-r.md`, Theorem 19) give trivial maps or a continuum of successors. A further rule *can* be a function of locked data alone and give off-curvelet stars: the Kretschmann rule’s stars are a readout of the germ. But **kinematics does not single out a dynamics**. What the germ does not supply is *which* readout, so a countable star is a new physical choice.

**The singleton-or-continuum dilemma.** In the target direction, the dilemma lives in the law of $R$. It is escaped only by leaving $R$ unspecified or by adding selection extras. That is a motivation problem, not a no-go theorem.

**Both readings smell of hidden variables.** Under the chance reading of §9, $R$ plus EPP1 is a stochastic hidden-variable jump process on $V$, the random walk of MMWI’s applied rule. Under the adopted co-existence reading, it has the Bohmian-typicality structure (Dürr–Goldstein–Zanghì 1992) that the first SHPMP 2008 referee named. Neither is a local hidden-variable theory that beats Bell. Equal weight $1/|B_R|$ is not implied by the mere existence of $R$.

## 9. EPP1, EPP2, and the product ansatz

**EPP1 (named postulate).** At a state $(O^u,a)$ where the hard gate holds, assign weight $1/|A(O^u,a)|$ to each arm. After a segment, that is $1/|B_R(O^u)|$. Its pedigree is the per-split rule, applied in MMWI (2006) and stated as SHPMP 2008’s Definition 3 (Appendix B), not `Strayhorn3`, which states path counting. It is adopted as a named postulate. Its domain is not determined, because no $R$ is adopted; the unadopted worked model has domain states (C11, C29). It is not implied by the bare existence of $R$, and it is not tuned to $|a|^2$. The first SHPMP referee read that programme, equal-share weights on an added state-transition layer with its own dynamics, as a hidden-variable programme and a cousin of Bohmian typicality; that reading stays attached (Appendix B, §8.5). The second referee’s objection, that the question inside Everett allows no added ontology, belongs to the frame this paper does not use.

**Reading the weight.**

- **Co-existence (typicality) reading, adopted as a working postulate (revisable).** The arms co-exist, and EPP1 is a measure on co-existing histories. It is the path measure that the EPP1 transition kernels define on completed histories (a product of kernels, not of independent factors): its finite-cut marginals are consistent by construction, and the Ionescu-Tulcea theorem (Kallenberg 2021, Theorem 8.24) extends them to completed histories.
  - *Cost.* It gives co-existing distinct evolutions **unequal** weight. Take the tree with root arms $A$ and $B$, three sub-arms under $A$ and one under $B$, cut at depth 2. Each $A_i$ gets $1/6$ and $B_1$ gets $1/2$, where Paper 1’s count gives $1/4$ each (C12). The co-existence *ontology* matches Paper 1; the *weighting* does not.
  - *Nearby readings.* This reading sits next to Bohmian typicality (Dürr–Goldstein–Zanghì 1992), and the hidden-variable smell attaches to it too. It is **not** AFLB 2008’s outcome counting: the arms are oriented observers joined by $R$, not decoherence branches (Paper 1’s own firewall), so this is not the Everett world-counting slot.
  - *Rideout–Sorkin.* Rideout–Sorkin sits beside this reading, since its measure lives on completed causets (Brightwell, Dowker, García, Henson and Sorkin 2003; classical sequential growth itself: Rideout and Sorkin 1999).
- **Chance reading, defined, not adopted.** One history is followed. At each decision it takes each arm of $A(O^u,a)$ with chance $1/|A(O^u,a)|$. This is a Markov jump rule on $(O^u,a)$: the random walk of MMWI’s applied rule. Its neighbours are Rideout–Sorkin classical sequential growth (a stochastic sequential process, the chance form of the same programme), Ghirardi–Rimini–Weber (Ghirardi, Rimini and Weber 1986), and Bell-type jump processes (Dürr, Goldstein, Tumulka and Zanghì 2005).

**Per-vertex weight versus Paper 1’s count.** Per-vertex weighting departs from Paper 1’s count, and any comparison must name its cut. Let $C$ be a finite cut: a set of nodes that every maximal path from the root, finite or infinite, meets exactly once. Depth cuts qualify, and proper-time cuts do once clause (2) holds (*Finite cuts*, below). Let $D(v)=\prod_{w<v}\deg(w)$ over the strict ancestors of $v$.
- Per-vertex weighting gives $1/D(v)$ on $C$, and the count gives $1/|C|$. **They agree on $C$ iff $D$ is constant on $C$**, and then $D\equiv|C|$.
  - *Proof.* Per-vertex weights on a finite cut sum to 1, since the cut meets every maximal path exactly once. So if $D\equiv d$ on $C$, then $|C|/d=1$, and $1/D(v)=1/|C|$ for every $v\in C$; conversely, agreement means $1/D(v)=1/|C|$ for every $v\in C$.
- With no dead end before depth $N$, they agree at every depth up to $N$ iff the tree is generation-regular to depth $N-1$ (with dead ends in the cut, only the "if" direction holds in general). A 12-leaf tree that is not generation-regular agrees at depth 3 and disagrees at depth 2, where the $A$-prefix gets $3/5$ by count and $1/2$ per vertex (C13). Agreement is not monotone in depth.
- At proper-time cuts the rules can disagree even on generation-regular trees (C14).
- The count’s value for a fixed prefix changes with the cut; the per-vertex value does not.
- *Dead ends.* A history that terminates before the cut is included in $C$, with $D$ taken along its actual length. This is consistent with unconditioned EPP1. The alternative, counting survivors only and conditioning per vertex on survival, can give a different verdict. Example: the root has arms $\alpha,\beta$; $\alpha$ ends; $\beta$ has two children; cut at depth 2. Counting the dead end, $C=\{\alpha,\beta_1,\beta_2\}$, per-vertex weights are $1/2,1/4,1/4$ against a count of $1/3$ each, so the rules disagree. Counting survivors only, per-vertex weights conditioned on survival are $1/2,1/2$, equal to the count.
- *Limit.* With finite stars, depth-$n$ counts converge to per-vertex weights on every cylinder set iff siblings are asymptotically balanced: $\mathrm{desc}_n(w)/\mathrm{desc}_n(w')\to1$ for all siblings $w,w'$, where $\mathrm{desc}_n(w)$ counts the nodes of the depth-$n$ cut below $w$ (dead ends included, as above; not the ancestor product $D$). Exact constancy of $D$ along a cofinal sequence of cuts makes the counts at those cuts equal the per-vertex weights, but it need not make the depth-$n$ counts converge (a binary root whose two subtrees have degrees $3,2,3,\dots$ and $2,3,2,\dots$ by depth has $D$ constant at every odd depth, while the $A$-prefix count alternates between $3/5$ and $1/2$); constancy at every depth from some depth on is sufficient. Neither is necessary (C15). C15's degrees grow without bound; with bounded degrees, balance without constant-$D$ cuts is also possible (if every $A$ node has children $B,A,A,A$, every $B$ node two children $B'$ and every $B'$ node eight children $A$, siblings are asymptotically balanced, yet no finite cut beyond depth 1 has constant $D$).
  - *Proof of the "iff".* For a node $v$ above depth $n$, $\mathrm{desc}_n(v)\ge1$, since dead ends stay in the cut. The depth-$n$ count gives the cylinder of $v$ the weight $\mathrm{desc}_n(v)/\mathrm{desc}_n(\mathrm{root})$. This is the product, along the path to $v$, of the shares $\mathrm{desc}_n(w)/\mathrm{desc}_n(p)=\mathrm{desc}_n(w)/\sum_{w'}\mathrm{desc}_n(w')$ of each child $w$ in its parent $p$ (the sum runs over the siblings $w'$ of $w$, $w$ included), while $1/D(v)$ is the product of the shares $1/\deg(p)$. If siblings are asymptotically balanced, each share tends to $1/\deg(p)$, since stars are finite, so every cylinder converges to its per-vertex weight. Conversely, if every cylinder converges, each share is the ratio of a child's and its parent's cylinder weights, whose limits are positive, so it tends to $1/\deg(p)$; with finitely many siblings that is asymptotic balance.
- *Finite cuts.* Once clause (2) holds for every history in the tree, König’s lemma (Kőnig 1927) makes the part of the tree before any proper-time cut finite when stars are finite. So both rules can be compared at a named $\tau$-cut (C29).

**A neighbour of the split: the maximal-entropy random walk.** Burda, Duda, Luck and Waclaw (2009). The generic random walk, with weight $1/\deg$ at each vertex, is the per-vertex form. MERW is defined so that all paths of a given length between given endpoints are equally likely. The depth-cut count from a fixed root matches MERW's transition law in general only in the long-path limit (C36); on some graphs, regular ones among them, it matches at every length. The generic random walk and MERW coincide on regular graphs and also on bipartite biregular ones, so regularity is a sufficient case, not the criterion (for a finite connected graph they coincide iff it is regular or bipartite biregular); on the tree of walks from a fixed root, the criterion is $D$-constancy at every depth, which there is equivalent to asymptotic sibling balance (*Limit*, above).

*Proof of the graph criterion.* On a finite connected graph with adjacency matrix $\mathcal A$, degrees $d_i$, leading eigenvalue $\lambda$ and positive leading eigenvector $\psi$, the generic random walk steps from $i$ to $j$ with probability $\mathcal A_{ij}/d_i$ and MERW with probability $\mathcal A_{ij}\psi_j/(\lambda\psi_i)$ (Burda et al. 2009). They coincide iff $\psi_j/\psi_i=\lambda/d_i$ on every edge $ij$. Applied to both orientations of an edge, this gives $d_id_j=\lambda^2$. So along any path the degrees alternate between some $d$ and $\lambda^2/d$. An odd cycle forces $d=\lambda^2/d$, and the graph is regular; with no odd cycle the graph is bipartite with constant degree on each side, that is, biregular. Conversely, on a regular graph $\psi$ is constant and $\lambda=d$; on a bipartite biregular graph with side degrees $q_1$ and $q_2$, $\lambda=\sqrt{q_1q_2}$, with $\psi=1$ on the side of degree $q_1$ and $\psi=\sqrt{q_2/q_1}$ on the other; in both cases the two walks coincide.

This carries a **hazard for (M2), not a Born claim.** What a Born-type claim through it would have to show is scoped in `notes/merw-litmus-scoping.md`. MERW's stationary density is $\psi_i^2$, the square of the leading eigenvector of the adjacency matrix. Duda relates that density to Born-rule probabilities (Duda 2011), and Faber obtains a Born-rule form and the stationary Schrödinger equation from MERW while arguing that quantum mechanics is not simply MERW (Faber 2024). With `Strayhorn3`'s path counting and its $|c_i|^2$ line in the pedigree, a referee may file (M2) alongside those readings. Nothing here adopts them, no Born route is taken, and the circularity wall of §10 stands.

**Named constraint, not a law: no jump dominance.** Suppose every history takes at least $n$ decisions and every decision has $m$ outgoing edges. Then under EPP1 the pure-geodesic history has weight $(1+m)^{-n}$ after $n$ decisions ($3^{-10}\approx1.69\times10^{-5}$ for $m=2$, $n=10$), and the number of jumps in the first $n$ decisions is exactly Binomial$(n,m/(1+m))$, since each decision jumps with weight $m/(1+m)$ independently of the past. If a history can stop deciding earlier, as under the rule as stated when a jump leaves the bound sector (its target geodesic plunges or is unbound, so no further vertex follows), the jump count is in general not Binomial, whether or not one conditions on survival. EPP1 weights cannot be tuned, and the continue arm has no privilege.

Under the Kretschmann rule as stated, typical histories are jump-dominated. Jumps are 2/3 of decisions as a ratio of expectations: $E[\text{jumps}]=\tfrac23E[\text{decisions}]$ by Wald's identity (Wald 1944), since $m=2$ at every bound decision, so under EPP1 each decision jumps with weight $2/3$ whatever came before. The identity needs a stopping rule with a finite expected number of decisions, such as a fixed cap on decisions (histories that leave the bound sector stop earlier); without a cap, finiteness is not shown. A single history's jump share differs from this ratio of expectations. At $c=0.1$, about 6.6% of histories remain bound after 20 decisions, and those that leave do so after a mean of 5.90 decisions (sampled, C34); at $c=0.01$ the orbit random-walks away from $[10,20]$ (qualitative; not pinned).

So if typical histories are to show geodesic motion over an observed window $[0,T]$, then $J(T)=\sum_{\text{decisions in }[0,T]}m_v/(1+m_v)$ must be small along typical histories. Each decision with $m_v\ge1$ contributes at least $1/2$. So any future $R$ must meet one of two conditions:
- **(J1)** vertices with $\mathrm{succ}_R\ne\emptyset$ are sparse: their number over observed windows is within tolerance; or
- **(J2)** jumps are observationally null: every target lies within a stated tolerance of the continuation in a named topology *on curvelets*, not merely on germs at the vertex.

The Kretschmann rule as stated fails both (C17). (J1) is window-dependent. The (a)-restriction meets it over late windows, since decisions end almost surely, and fails it over early ones: jumps are 1/2 of the decisions that occur as a ratio of expectations (Wald's identity applies, the expected number of decisions being finite: 27.68 at $c=0.01$, exact; at most 57 decisions in 20,000 sampled histories; C33). It fails (J2): one jump changes the orbit by $O(1)$. This is recorded as a constraint on any future $R$, not as a demand to write one.

**Exhaustion.** If countable stars are ever allowed, the exhaustion must be named. The default is finite stars.

**EPP2 (foil).** EPP2 weights an arm in proportion to its post-branch ensemble size, $|E_{\mathcal W}|$ or $|E_M|$ (§4; see the product ansatz below). An arm's post-branch ensemble needs an arm-to-ensemble map. No such map is fixed here. Nor is it fixed whether $E_M$ counts a manifold once or with multiplicity; with multiplicity, $|E_M|=|E_{\mathcal W}|$ and the two sizes coincide. Weighting an arm by its ensemble size is an ensemble vote. It would be a **departure from Paper 1 if promoted**, because in Paper 1 each distinct evolution counts as one and extra weighting is new theory. Its form is SHPMP 2008's Definition 5 (Appendix B).

**Product ansatz (Hope).** If post-branch ensembles factored with a shared reservoir, EPP2 might collapse toward EPP1. The hope fails if the measured distinction locks other topology, or if the arms admit different external structure. Even then $\infty/\infty$ needs a named exhaustion. This is not Stoica (2025): $|\Psi|$ is not in the count. SHPMP's Axioms 2 and 3 are the 2008 precedent for the agreement, where it was postulated (Appendix B). Here it is a Hope, not an axiom.

## 10. Bell: Obs-locality, and what a no-no-go sketch would need (none exhibited)

A *no-no-go* would be an argument that Bell-type no-go theorems do not by themselves exclude this framework. It would not be a claim that those theorems are false. **No such sketch is exhibited here.** A digraph fact is not a Bell theorem. This paper does not refute Bell, does not derive Born, and does not claim a local hidden-variable dynamics that beats Bell.

A real sketch would need at least:
1. two observers as vertices;
2. an embedding of both in one world with spacelike-separated anchors, which selects an ensemble member and so is E-smuggling;
3. a correlation rule with setting dependence;
4. a named Bell premise.

A rule is *Obs-local* if the arm set and weights at a state $(O^u,a)$ depend only on that state and $R$, not on any other observer’s vertex and not on a world embedding both. **The state carries one bit of history data, the arrival flag $a$; §4’s lock-side definition excludes history data, so the flag is not lock-side.** The observer is $O^u$, Paper 1’s object; the flag belongs to the process state. The flag’s only effect is to suppress branching at jump-reached states, so the question it raises is whether EPP1 applies, not what weights it gives when it does. Which way to resolve this is an open call (T1, §11); none is taken here.

The Hope is that Obs-local rules are compatible with Bell’s theorem. Nothing here shows it. Under the chance reading, and under the mapping in which each wing is an observer evolving by its own rule, an Obs-local rule is a local stochastic process for each observer, which is what Bell’s theorem constrains, so any route for this Hope would run through the co-existence reading. The observer’s germ fixes a whole analytic patch, so the mapping is not automatic; it is kept open (*Mathematical Foundations* §9; Problem note 12). Locality in $V$ is not Bell's locality: Bell's premise is stated in spacetime, and nothing here makes an Obs-local rule Bell-local in spacetime. It is not a claim that Bell is refuted or evaded, and the list above says what a sketch would need.

The circularity wall stands. A tree drawn by hand on $(V,R)$ to match $|a|^2$ is Paper 1’s trivial scheme, not a derivation. In Paper 1’s words (`papers/observer-space-ontology.md`, §8): “You cannot define the count by the Born weight and then call the count a justification of the Born weight.”

**Both rules as counts at a cut.** EPP1 is the count at the depth-1 cut anchored at the current state; (M2) counts at a distant named cut. Each fact comes with a cost. A count anchored afresh at the current state, at any fixed depth, gives weights that are functions of that state: depth 1 reads only the current arms (EPP1); a deeper anchor also reads $R$ ahead (on a finite strongly connected graph, MERW in the long-path limit, C36). What is particular to depth 1 is that it reads nothing of $R$ beyond the current arms and needs no named depth. State-function weights fit Problem note 6, but EPP1 is a named postulate and a deeper anchor needs a named depth; either strains note 9. A distant fixed cut is what gives co-existing distinct evolutions equal weight, as in Paper 1. Neither fact is used to decide; the ruling (open-calls row) is David's.

**How the (a) transient bears on T3 (not resolved).** Under the (a)-restriction on the instance, jumps are **finitely many per history** (almost surely one at $c=0.1$, and 12 to 14 at $c=0.01$, C33). ## Appendix B. Pedigree of the two measure rules

**Pedigree.** The author’s 2005–06 corpus contains both measure rules, used side by side and never separated.
- The manuscript “General Relativity and the Probability Interpretation of Everett’s Relative State Formulation” (MMWI, dated 24 January 2006) **states** the count: “each of these alternative $N_O$ evolutions of the observer is attributed an equal probability $1/N_O$” (p. 11).
- The same manuscript **applies** the per-split rule in the argument attached to its Fig. 1E (PDF p. 16): “each time the diagram ‘splits’ into two directions, each of the two trajectories is attributed an equal probability measure”. That applied rule, a random walk on an uneven tree, is the pedigree of per-vertex EPP1 and of the chance reading (§9). The quantity that argument branches on, MMWI’s path count, is not used here.
- The draft `Strayhorn3`, compiled 15 October 2006 (PDF metadata), **states path counting**: “the relative probability of evolution to point $p_i$ is proportional to the number of distinct paths linking $p$ to $p_i$” (PDF p. 4). It then writes $|c_i|^2=\langle p,p_i\rangle$ from it (PDF p. 6). That is the form of Paper 1’s count, not of EPP1. Its abstract says that a Born derivation was presented in the 2006 Philica article it cites; this paper makes no such claim and cites `Strayhorn3` only for the form of its rule.
- AFLB 2008 counts $2^M$ worlds on a generation-regular tree, where the two rules coincide. It cannot tell them apart.
- The 2008 SHPMP submission (31 March 2008; rejected 1 July 2008) separates the two rules explicitly. Its Definition 2 is the count (“proportional to the number of branches associated with that outcome at the end of the experiment”). Its Definition 3 is the per-split rule (“each branch receives 1/N of the share of probability measure from its parent branch”), read as a classical stochastic state machine. It shows that they disagree on one tree, rejects the count in its simple form because its answer changes with the slice at which branches are counted, adopts the per-split rule, and keeps a count on a refined sub-branch tree (its Definition 4). Its Definition 5, weight in proportion to the worlds in each branch’s ensemble, is the form of EPP2. SHPMP did not simply choose the per-split rule over the counts; it postulated agreement. Axiom 1 ties Definition 1 to Definition 3 (printed p. 17). Axiom 2 ties Definition 4 to Definition 3 (printed p. 18). Having noted that counting every world equally would bring back the count it had rejected, it adds Axiom 3, “There is one (and only one) Everettian world for each Everettian sub-branch” (printed p. 28), which ties Definition 5 to Definition 4. So in 2008 the EPP2 form equalled the EPP1 form **by axiom** (Axioms 2 and 3 together). That is the 2008 precedent for the agreement the product-ansatz Hope looks for; it was reached by postulate, not by a factorisation of ensembles, and here it stays a Hope (§9). That submission argued inside the Everett-versus-decision-theory frame; this paper does not, and cites it for its rules, axioms and tree argument, not for that frame.

So the 2005–06 manuscripts used both rules without separating them, SHPMP 2008 separated them, rejected the simple count, took the per-split rule as its transition rule and tied its other counts to it by axiom, and Paper 1 went back to the count. EPP1 takes the 2008 rule as its working postulate and (M2) would take Paper 1’s; David has ruled (M1) at L3 (§11), and §9 gives the general criterion that separates them. The type-(ii) pad is this transition / random-walk hypothesis under a new name. That makes the first 2008 SHPMP referee’s hidden-variable reading concrete, and it stays loud (§8.5, §12).
